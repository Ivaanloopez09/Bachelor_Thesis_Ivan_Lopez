# Bachelor's Thesis: Möbius Transformations and the Denjoy–Wolff Theorem

This repository contains my **Bachelor's Thesis (Treball de Final de Grau - TFG)** in Mathematics, completed at the **Universitat de València (UV)**.

> 🌐 **Language Note:** The written manuscript and documentation provided in this thesis are written in **Catalan**.

## 📌 Overview

This project explores the rich mathematical structure of **Möbius transformations**. While fundamentally **analytical** in nature, the thesis adopts an interdisciplinary approach, incorporating tools and perspectives from:

* **Complex Analysis:** Dynamics of analytic self-maps of the unit disk and classical complex function theory.

* **Group Theory:** The algebraic group structure of Möbius transformations, automorphism groups, and matrix representations ($PSL(2, \mathbb{C})$).

* **Pseudohyperbolic Geometry:** The Schwarz–Pick Lemma shows that holomorphic self-maps of the unit disk are contractions with respect to the pseudohyperbolic metric. This provides a deep geometric interpretation of results that are fundamentally analytical in nature.

The **unit disk** ($\mathbb{D} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : \vert{}z\vert{} < 1\}$) plays a central role throughout the work, serving as the core domain for studying iteration dynamics and invariant metrics.

## 🎯 Key Highlights & Focus

* **Schwarz–Pick Lemma & Geometric Insights:** Detailed examination of how holomorphic self-maps act as contractions on the pseudohyperbolic metric, linking analytical properties to geometric invariances. Specifically, automorphisms of the disk emerge naturally as the isometric maps that strictly preserve this distance.

* **Centrality of the Unit Disk:** Deep dive into automorphisms of the unit disk $\text{Aut}(\mathbb{D})$ and their classification into elliptic, parabolic, hyperbolic, and loxodromic types based on fixed-point behavior.

* **Self-Contained Denjoy–Wolff Theorem:** A core contribution of the thesis is a complete, self-contained proof of the **Denjoy–Wolff Theorem** specifically adapted for **Möbius transformations**, avoiding unnecessary abstract machinery while keeping full mathematical rigor.

* **Iterative Dynamics:** Analysis of the convergence behavior of sequences of iterates $f^n(z)$ for disk endomorphisms, establishing the existence and attraction properties of the Denjoy–Wolff point.

## 📖 Structure of the Thesis

1. **Introduction & Preliminaries:** Fundamental concepts in complex analysis, metric topology, and functional analysis.

2. **Algebraic & Geometric Aspects:** Group actions, matrix representations, invariant metrics, and the Schwarz–Pick Lemma.

3. **Möbius Transformations on the Unit Disk:** Classification, fixed points, and dynamical properties.

4. **The Denjoy–Wolff Theorem for Möbius Transformations:** A rigorous, self-contained proof and comprehensive analysis of fixed-point dynamics and asymptotic behavior under iteration.
